# mod-406 — Model Gateway and Routing for Agent Workloads

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours · **Position in track:** sixth module (after [`mod-401`](../mod-401-agent-platform-position-and-paved-road-philosophy), [`mod-402`](../mod-402-agent-runtime-service-and-durable-execution), [`mod-403`](../mod-403-mcp-registry-and-tool-gateway), [`mod-404`](../mod-404-sandboxed-code-execution-plane), [`mod-405`](../mod-405-tool-and-permission-policy-plane)).

The runtime (mod-402) owns the agent loop. The MCP gateway (mod-403) owns the tools. The sandbox (mod-404) runs tool code. The policy plane (mod-405) authorises what the agent does. That leaves one hot-path hop on every step: the call out to a model. Where does that call go? Which provider and model, which region and key, under which tenant's quota, cached against which prefix, carrying which obligation to redact or validate? That is the slice this module owns. By the end you can architect a multi-provider gateway with fallback and retry tuned to tool-calling loops, enforce per-tenant quotas and burst budgets with correct cost attribution, coordinate prompt caching without leaking context across tenants, apply structured-output and PII obligations at the gateway rather than in every agent, and defend a build-vs-buy call across LiteLLM, Portkey, Envoy AI Gateway, Kong AI Gateway, and Cloudflare AI Gateway — and hand off to `ai-infra-inference-engineer` for the self-hosted inference substrate the gateway may be routing to.

## Learning objectives

- Architect a model gateway for agent workloads — multi-provider routing (OpenAI / Anthropic / Google / self-hosted), fallback and retry policies tuned for tool-calling loops, per-tenant quotas, burst budgets, token / cost attribution.
- Compare LiteLLM, Portkey, Envoy AI Gateway, Kong AI Gateway, and Cloudflare AI Gateway on routing semantics, observability shape, and deployment model; defend a build-vs-buy decision.
- Engineer prompt-cache coordination across tenants (prefix hashing, cache partitioning, cache-affinity routing) so savings accrue without leaking context.
- Enforce structured-output schemas and PII redaction at the gateway rather than in every agent.
- Hand off to `ai-infra-inference-engineer` (level 35) for the inference-serving substrate (vLLM / SGLang / TRT-LLM) the gateway routes to.

## How to approach this module

1. Read the seven chapters in order. The slice (01) is the frame everything else fits in; routing (02) and retry (03) are the forward and error paths; quotas (04) and cache (05) are the tenant-facing state the plane coordinates; schema and redaction (06) are the two obligations the gateway enforces centrally; the build-vs-buy call and the inference handoff (07) close the module.
2. Do exercise 01 before reading chapter 05. The cache's affinity router extends the candidate walk you build in exercise 01; reading about cache coordination without having walked the candidates once is abstract.
3. Do exercise 03 before reading chapter 07. The cross-tenant leakage test suite in exercise 03 is the concrete form of the "must-own" tier-1 capability in chapter 07; walking the test before reading the portfolio frame makes the portfolio frame make sense.
4. Mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on any claim you cannot verify against the current provider documentation, pricing pages, or gateway-tool release notes. The model-gateway surface moves faster than any other module in the track; the flag convention is the same as mod-403 / 404 / 405 — it blocks auto-merge, and that is cheaper than silently guessing.
5. Keep the chapter 04 billing-event schema open while reading chapters 05 and 06. Every obligation chapter 06 lands on a request must leave a trace on the event; the cache outcome from chapter 05 is a field on the event; the price-regression alarm from chapter 04 reads the events chapters 05 and 06 populate.

## Where the handoffs land

- **`ai-infra-inference-engineer` (level 35)** — owns the vLLM / SGLang / TensorRT-LLM substrate the gateway routes to for self-hosted inference. Your contract to them is an OpenAI-compatible wire shape, published capability declarations, request-tagging for their scheduler, workload-identity auth, and cache-affinity hints. Their contract to you is stable token-count reporting, deterministic error classes, published changelog, and OpenTelemetry gen-ai spans. Chapter 07 details the contract.
- **`ai-risk-engineer` (level 25)** — authors the harm model you consume via the policy plane's redaction obligations (chapter 06). Your contract is to apply every redaction obligation they name as a gateway-side enforcement, to emit decision evidence on every match, and to refuse requests whose egress would violate a hard-class obligation.
- **`agentic-safety-engineer` (level 40)** — ships red-team findings that often point at prompt-injection-driven budget loops or egress attempts. Your contract is to accept their findings as test cases against the budget guards (chapter 04) and against the redaction detectors (chapter 06), and to surface the quota-refusal and detector-match metrics in a form they can assess.
- **`agentic-ai-engineer` (level 30) and `senior-agentic-ai-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — write the agents that call through the gateway. Your contract is a stable internal request shape (chapter 02), predictable retry semantics (chapter 03), structured deny-reason codes (chapter 04), and the paved-road SDK (mod-412) that speaks the gateway's wire shape with the right ergonomics.
- **`ai-infra-sre` (peer) and your own future mod-410 work** — consume the model-gateway SLIs (fallback rate per pair, cache hit rate per namespace, quota-refusal rate per tenant, price-regression alarm, circuit-state transitions) and own the incident cadence when a provider degrades. Their SLO proposals become your admission ceilings and routing-health thresholds.
- **`platform-finops` (and whatever name your organisation gives the equivalent)** — consumes the billing-event stream (chapter 04) as the single source of truth for chargeback, showback, and provider-invoice reconciliation. Your contract is the schema's split-token fields, the per-attempt list, and the versioned price table; their contract is to treat your stream as authoritative and reconcile the provider invoice against it rather than the other way around.
- **`agentic-systems-architect` (peer)** — pairs with you on the build-vs-buy call (chapter 07). Vendor tools in this space move quickly; the quarterly review the chapter names is a joint responsibility.
