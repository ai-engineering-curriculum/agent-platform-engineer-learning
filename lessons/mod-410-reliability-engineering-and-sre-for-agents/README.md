# mod-410 — Reliability Engineering and SRE for the Agent Platform

**Estimated effort:** 12 hours · **Position in track:** tenth module (after [`mod-401`](../mod-401-agent-platform-position-and-paved-road-philosophy), [`mod-402`](../mod-402-agent-runtime-service-and-durable-execution), [`mod-403`](../mod-403-mcp-registry-and-tool-gateway), [`mod-404`](../mod-404-sandboxed-code-execution-plane), [`mod-405`](../mod-405-tool-and-permission-policy-plane), [`mod-406`](../mod-406-model-gateway-and-routing), [`mod-407`](../mod-407-evaluation-platform-and-regression-gating), [`mod-408`](../mod-408-observability-and-opentelemetry-for-agents), [`mod-409`](../mod-409-agent-to-agent-routing-and-service-mesh)).

Every prior module in the track built a plane and left behind signals, levers, and a scoping story. The runtime (mod-402) emits session-completion events. The MCP gateway (mod-403) emits tool-health signals. The sandbox (mod-404) emits saturation. The policy plane (mod-405) emits authorisation decisions. The model gateway (mod-406) emits routing, token, cost, and provider-outage records. The eval plane (mod-407) emits verdicts on fixtures. The observability plane (mod-408) emits a consistent `gen_ai.*` and `platform.*` semantic stream. The inter-agent plane (mod-409) emits resilience state. This module asks a different question from any of them: given all those signals, how does the platform *operate* under production load, and what does "healthy enough" mean when the thing you are operating is non-deterministic, long-running, and dependent on providers that do not publish their outage calendars?

That is the slice this module owns. By the end you can define the five SLIs the Agent Platform Engineer role must measure on every agent workload (session-completion rate, time-to-first-tool, time-to-final-answer, hallucination rate, safety-refusal rate), set SLO targets per workload class and resilience tier grounded in the Google SRE Book and Workbook, engineer an error-budget policy as an opt-in paved-road contract that stream-aligned teams sign when they adopt the platform, run chaos drills against the four agent-dependency failure classes (model-provider outage, MCP flake, sandbox exhaustion, eval-infra degradation) with scored scorecards, author incident-response playbooks for the four agent-specific failure modes (runaway loop, context exhaustion, prompt-injection containment, tool-cascade meltdown), and close the post-incident loop by promoting reproducible failures into the mod-407 catalogue so the gate blocks the next deploy that would reintroduce them.

## Learning objectives

- Define SLIs and SLOs for agent workloads — session completion rate, time-to-first-tool, time-to-final-answer, hallucination rate, safety-refusal rate — grounded in the [Google SRE Book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) and [SRE Workbook](https://sre.google/workbook/table-of-contents/) model.
- Engineer error-budget policies that stream-aligned teams opt into when they adopt the paved road, with mechanical actions on each burn-rate band.
- Run chaos drills for agent dependencies — model-provider outage, MCP-server flake, sandbox exhaustion, eval-infra degradation — and measure platform response with scored scorecards.
- Author incident-response playbooks for agent-specific failure modes — runaway agent loops, context-window exhaustion, prompt-injection containment, tool-cascade meltdown — each with pre-agreed, named containment levers.
- Feed post-incident regression fixtures back into the evaluation platform (mod-407) so recurrences are blocked at the gate.

## How to approach this module

1. Read the six chapters in order. The slice (01) is the frame; SLIs and SLOs (02) are the measurement layer; the error-budget policy (03) is the operating contract over the measurements; chaos drills (04) and incident playbooks (05) are the practice-and-response pair; the post-incident fixture loop (06) closes the module back onto mod-407.
2. Do exercise 01 before reading chapter 03. Writing a real SLO document and getting one to turn green makes the error-budget policy's "burning what" question concrete rather than abstract.
3. Do exercise 03 before exercise 04. The chaos-drill scorecard is the structural skeleton of the incident post-mortem; running one drill scorecard makes the incident playbook's scorecard section obvious.
4. Keep the chapter 01 `ReliabilityRecord` shape open while reading the rest. Every chapter adds a field; losing the record as the connective tissue is how the plane devolves into disconnected dashboards and a Slack channel.
5. Mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on any claim you cannot verify. The specific burn-rate thresholds from the SRE Workbook, the fault-injection CRD field names, the model-provider outage SLA numbers, and the OWASP LLM Top 10 category labels all shift version to version; the flag convention is consistent with mod-403 / 404 / 405 / 406 / 407 / 408 / 409 and blocks auto-merge, which is cheaper than silently guessing.

## Where the handoffs land

- **`senior-agentic-ai-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — owns the agent-side implementation of the fixes this plane catches. The plane's contract to them is "the SLO document is a thing you sign, the policy is a thing we exercise, the playbook is a thing we exercise *for you* when the pager fires, and the post-incident fixture is a thing we author with you". Their contract to the plane is "I will not bypass the gate" and "my agent emits the SDK's required attributes" (cross-reference mod-412).
- **`ai-eval-engineer` (peer, level 30, mod-407)** — co-owner of the hallucination SLO (chapter 02's judge calibration) and the fixture catalogue (chapter 06's promotion target). The plane's contract is "we submit fixtures with proper provenance and rubric"; their contract is "the catalogue schema and the judge calibration will be stable between quarterly reviews".
- **`agentic-safety-engineer` (peer, level 40, mod-411)** — co-owner of the safety-refusal SLO (chapter 02's drift alert) and the prompt-injection playbook (chapter 05 playbook 3). The plane's contract is "we page you on the cross-cutting signals and preserve evidence"; their contract is "the guardrail rule set catches the pattern long-term".
- **`model-evaluation-engineer` (peer, level 30)** — reviewer on fixture rubrics when the fixture asserts a statistical property. The plane's contract is "we involve you on rubrics that need power analysis or CI design"; their contract is "the rubric methodology is documented and reusable".
- **`ai-infra-inference-engineer` (peer, level 35)** — owns the long-horizon capacity picture. The plane consumes their utilisation and throughput signals for the sandbox-exhaustion drill (chapter 04 class 3); they consume the plane's incident records when a capacity decision correlates with an SLO miss.
- **`agent-platform-leadership` (mod-413)** — owns the exception register for error-budget overrides, the quarterly policy review, the chaos-drill approval for production scope, and the reliability-posture report cadence. The plane is the mechanism; leadership is the accountability.
- **Observability plane (mod-408)** — upstream dependency on the semantic vocabulary and trace propagation that every SLI formula reads from. Every SLI in chapter 02 is a query over this plane's semantic contract.
- **Inter-agent plane (mod-409)** — upstream dependency on the circuit-breaker and retry-budget mechanisms that the tool-cascade playbook (chapter 05 playbook 4) and the class-2 chaos drill (chapter 04) exercise.
- **Model gateway (mod-406) and MCP gateway (mod-403)** — upstream dependencies on the provider-outage records (chapter 03's provider-budget split) and the chaos-mode admin surfaces (chapter 04 class 1 and class 2).
