# mod-411 — Safety Plane and Guardrails as a Service

**Estimated effort:** 12 hours · **Position in track:** eleventh module (after [`mod-401`](../mod-401-agent-platform-position-and-paved-road-philosophy), [`mod-402`](../mod-402-agent-runtime-service-and-durable-execution), [`mod-403`](../mod-403-mcp-registry-and-tool-gateway), [`mod-404`](../mod-404-sandboxed-code-execution-plane), [`mod-405`](../mod-405-tool-and-permission-policy-plane), [`mod-406`](../mod-406-model-gateway-and-routing), [`mod-407`](../mod-407-evaluation-platform-and-regression-gating), [`mod-408`](../mod-408-observability-and-opentelemetry-for-agents), [`mod-409`](../mod-409-agent-to-agent-routing-and-service-mesh), [`mod-410`](../mod-410-reliability-engineering-and-sre-for-agents)).

Every prior module left behind a seam the safety plane runs on. The runtime (mod-402) emits turn events. The MCP gateway (mod-403) carries tool invocations. The sandbox (mod-404) runs code. The policy plane (mod-405) answers "is this principal allowed to call this tool". The model gateway (mod-406) brokers completions. The evaluation plane (mod-407) catalogues regression fixtures. The observability plane (mod-408) fixes the semantic vocabulary and the post-market-monitoring picture. The inter-agent plane (mod-409) carries agent-to-agent calls. The SRE plane (mod-410) runs the fleet. This module asks the question none of them finish: *is the content of this interaction acceptable* — is this input safe to feed to the model, is this output safe to return to the user, is this tool call safe to execute, and when the answer is "no", what does the refusal look like.

That is the slice this module owns. By the end you can design the safety plane as a shared service with three hooks (input filter, output validator, tool-invocation policy hook) and one refusal shape, compose [NeMo Guardrails](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/guardrails/latest/index.html), [Guardrails AI](https://www.guardrailsai.com/docs), [Llama Guard](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/llama-guard-llm-based-input-output-safeguard-for-human-ai-conversations/), [ShieldGemma](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/shieldgemma/model_card), [OpenAI Moderation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/moderation), [Anthropic Constitutional Classifiers](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers), [Lakera Guard](https://www.lakera.ai/), and [Presidio](https://microsoft.github.io/presidio/) behind one plane, map the [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/) and the [OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/agentic-ai-threats-and-mitigations/) catalogue onto that plane's primitives, defend against direct and indirect prompt injection (spotlighting, classifier calibration, tool-invocation safety net), emit a standardised `SafetyRecord` schema that the AI risk register and the [EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) Article 15 / 72 post-market-monitoring export both consume without transformation, measure the plane's effectiveness against the red-team corpus `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) and `agentic-safety-engineer` (level 40) maintain, and reason about build-vs-buy across the vendor landscape (Robust Intelligence, Lakera, Arthur, HiddenLayer, Calypso) vs. the OSS stack.

## Learning objectives

- Engineer the platform's safety plane as a shared service — input filters, output validators, tool-invocation policy enforcement, standardised refusal behaviour — built on [NeMo Guardrails](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/guardrails/latest/index.html), [Guardrails AI](https://www.guardrailsai.com/docs), [Llama Guard](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/llama-guard-llm-based-input-output-safeguard-for-human-ai-conversations/), [ShieldGemma](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/shieldgemma/model_card), [OpenAI Moderation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/moderation), [Anthropic Constitutional Classifiers](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers), [Lakera Guard](https://www.lakera.ai/), and [Presidio](https://microsoft.github.io/presidio/).
- Defend against [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/) and [OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/agentic-ai-threats-and-mitigations/) at platform scope — prompt injection, indirect injection, excessive agency, insecure output handling, sensitive-info disclosure, tool misuse.
- Measure guardrail effectiveness against red-team findings produced by `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) and `agentic-safety-engineer` (level 40) — this role operates the plane; those peers author the methodology.
- Emit a standardised safety-event schema that `ai-risk-engineer` consumes for the AI risk register and that satisfies [EU AI Act Article 15](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/15/) and [Article 72](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/72/) post-market-monitoring obligations.
- Reason about build-vs-buy for guardrail vendors ([Robust Intelligence](https://www.robustintelligence.com/), [Lakera](https://www.lakera.ai/), [Arthur](https://www.arthur.ai/), [HiddenLayer](https://hiddenlayer.com/), [Calypso](https://calypsoai.com/)) vs. open-source guardrails behind the platform's safety plane.

## How to approach this module

1. Read the seven chapters in order. The slice (01) is the frame; the toolkit landscape (02) is the engine map; the OWASP mapping (03) is the identifier set the plane commits to; prompt-injection defence (04) is the first-class threat; the schema (05) is the stream shape downstream consumers join to; effectiveness measurement (06) is how the plane proves it works; build-vs-buy (07) is the decision the plane re-visits every quarter.
2. Do exercise 01 before reading chapter 04. Standing up a stub input filter against a real classifier (even a mock one) makes chapter 04's calibration conversation concrete rather than abstract.
3. Do exercise 03 after chapter 05 — the schema is the artefact the exercise commits to. The two reinforce each other; writing the schema file before the chapter is read will land you with a loose shape that exercise 04 pays for.
4. Keep the chapter 01 `SafetyRecord` shape open while reading the rest. Every chapter adds a field; losing the record as the connective tissue is how the plane devolves into disconnected classifier endpoints and a Slack channel.
5. Mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on any claim you cannot verify. The specific OWASP LLM Top 10 rev strings, EU AI Act Article wording, specific vendor pricing, engine model-version identifiers, and the Chaos Mesh-like CRD names in the chapter-02 operational section all shift version-to-version. The flag convention is consistent with mod-403 / 404 / 405 / 406 / 407 / 408 / 409 / 410 and blocks auto-merge, which is cheaper than silently guessing.

## Where the handoffs land

- **`ai-risk-engineer` (peer, level 25)** — owns the AI risk register and the lower-complexity red-team corpus. The plane's contract to them is "the `SafetyRecord` stream is the register's primary ingest and the schema is stable within a major version"; their contract to the plane is "the corpus is versioned, the rows carry OWASP tags, and the quarterly-signed scorecard is co-produced". Cross-reference exercise 04.
- **`agentic-safety-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — owns the harder, agent-specific red-team methodology — multi-turn setups, indirect-injection chains, tool-misuse patterns, A2A poisoning, misalignment probes. The plane's contract is "we replay your findings on each policy-pack change and sign the scorecard with you"; their contract is "the methodology produces reproducible rows the harness can replay". Cross-reference chapter 06 and exercise 04.
- **`senior-agentic-ai-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — owns the agent-side implementation. The plane's contract to them is "importing the paved-road SDK (mod-412) opts you into the hooks with no manual wiring"; their contract to the plane is "your agent does not bypass the hooks and your system prompt treats user / retrieved / tool-result content as untrusted". Cross-reference chapter 04 and mod-412.
- **`ai-eval-engineer` (peer, level 30, mod-407)** — owns the fixture catalogue and the judge calibration the plane relies on for groundedness. The plane's contract is "we promote reproducible safety failures as `safety/<finding_id>` fixtures with provenance"; their contract is "the catalogue schema is stable and the fixture runs produce verdicts the plane can gate deploys on". Cross-reference chapter 06.
- **Policy plane (mod-405)** — owns identity-and-permission. The safety plane's tool-invocation hook runs *after* mod-405's allow. Cross-reference chapter 01 and chapter 03.
- **SRE plane (mod-410)** — owns the safety-refusal-rate SLI and its drift alert. The safety plane produces the `SafetyRecord` stream the SLI queries; the SRE plane produces the paging on drift. Cross-reference mod-410 chapter 02 and chapter 06.
- **Observability plane (mod-408)** — upstream dependency on the `platform.safety.*` attribute namespace and the content-store retention. Cross-reference chapter 05 and mod-408 chapter 06 (EU AI Act Article 72 broader picture).
- **Model gateway (mod-406)** and **MCP gateway (mod-403)** — upstream dependencies on the completion-return and tool-invocation seams. The plane's hooks are the middleware on those seams.
- **mod-412 (paved-road SDK)** — packages the three hook invocations so a stream-aligned team's agent is on the plane by importing the library.
- **mod-413 (platform leadership)** — owns the quarterly scorecard operating cadence, the policy-pack change-control board, the exception register when a tenant requests a temporary pack override, and the build-vs-buy decision-document rev sign-offs.

## Scope policing

The plane does **not** own: identity-and-permission (mod-405), red-team methodology (`ai-risk-engineer`, `agentic-safety-engineer`), the risk register itself, the eval fixture catalogue (mod-407), the trace vocabulary (mod-408), product-side agent correctness (`senior-agentic-ai-engineer`), capacity planning (`ai-infra-inference-engineer`), the SDK's own hook invocation code (mod-412), governance signing authority (mod-413), or legal interpretation (counsel). Drift off this slice and the plane becomes "the people who put the moderation library into every agent" — a shared library, not a plane.
