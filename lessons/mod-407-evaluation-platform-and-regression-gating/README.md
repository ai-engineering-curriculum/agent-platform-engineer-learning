# mod-407 — Shared Evaluation Platform and Regression Gating

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours · **Position in track:** seventh module (after [`mod-401`](../mod-401-agent-platform-position-and-paved-road-philosophy), [`mod-402`](../mod-402-agent-runtime-service-and-durable-execution), [`mod-403`](../mod-403-mcp-registry-and-tool-gateway), [`mod-404`](../mod-404-sandboxed-code-execution-plane), [`mod-405`](../mod-405-tool-and-permission-policy-plane), [`mod-406`](../mod-406-model-gateway-and-routing)).

The runtime (mod-402) produces agent interactions. The MCP gateway (mod-403) produces tool-call outcomes. The sandbox (mod-404) runs the code. The policy plane (mod-405) authorises what the agent does. The model gateway (mod-406) produces routing, token, and cost facts on every call. Every one of those planes shipped a decision today — a new prompt, a new tool, a new router entry, a new quota, a new policy. Which of those decisions makes the fleet better, which makes it worse, and which is a silent regression nobody will notice until it hurts a customer? That is the slice this module owns. By the end you can author an evaluation-harness template library over Inspect and Promptfoo so a stream-aligned team onboards a new agent eval in hours, engineer the dataset management plane (versioning, provenance, synthetic-data labelling, access control), stand up a trace / replay store that makes regression investigation a one-command query, and wire eval-gated CI/CD against SWE-bench / τ-bench / GAIA / AgentBench and per-team custom fixtures — and hand off cleanly to `ai-eval-engineer` for application-layer eval methodology and to `model-evaluation-engineer` for benchmark and statistical methodology.

## Learning objectives

- Build an evaluation-harness template library on top of Inspect and Promptfoo so stream-aligned teams onboard a new agent eval in hours, not weeks.
- Engineer the eval dataset management plane — dataset versioning, provenance, synthetic-data labelling, access control.
- Stand up the trace / replay store that captures every agent interaction and makes regression investigation routine.
- Wire eval-gated CI/CD hooks that block bad deploys against standard regression fixtures (SWE-bench, τ-bench, GAIA, AgentBench) plus per-team custom fixtures.
- Hand off to `ai-eval-engineer` (level 30) for the methodology this platform templatises (judge calibration, RAG eval, online eval) and to `model-evaluation-engineer` (level 30) for statistical / benchmark methodology depth.

## How to approach this module

1. Read the six chapters in order. The slice (01) is the frame; the harness library (02), dataset plane (03), and trace store (04) are the three data planes the rest sits on; eval-gated CI (05) is the policy layer over the three; the handoffs (06) close the module.
2. Do exercise 01 before reading chapter 04. The template's trace-sink wiring is the simplest introduction to the trace-store contract chapter 04 formalises; building the sink stub first grounds the chapter's schema.
3. Do exercise 04 before reading chapter 05. The gate's reference to `catalogue://<fixture>@<version>` is exercise 04's output; walking the catalogue publish → fetch → gate flow once makes chapter 05's gate record make sense as a consumer of concrete inputs rather than an abstract schema.
4. Mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on any claim you cannot verify against current framework documentation, benchmark publication dates, or provider pricing. The eval-surface moves fast — benchmark revisions, Inspect / Promptfoo feature sets, and the standard-fixture leaderboards shift quarter over quarter; the flag convention is the same as mod-403 / 404 / 405 / 406 — it blocks auto-merge, and that is cheaper than silently guessing.
5. Keep the chapter 01 `EvalRecord` shape open while reading chapters 02–05. Every piece they build adds a field to the record; losing the record as the connective tissue is how the plane devolves into disconnected tools.

## Where the handoffs land

- **`ai-eval-engineer` (peer, level 30)** — owns application-layer eval methodology. Your contract to them is a hash-pinned rubric registry, a judge router with a dedicated budget bucket, a flaky-fixture / override-frequency alert stream, and the per-team custom fixtures surfaced on the catalogue. Their contract to you is the rubric content, the calibration cards (judge agreement rate against humans), the judge-prompt change-control review, and the online-eval sampling policy. Chapter 06 details the six handoffs.
- **`model-evaluation-engineer` (peer, level 30)** — owns benchmark construction and statistical rigour. Your contract to them is the catalogue (to host their fixtures), the template library (to run them), and the ingest-time contamination warnings. Their contract to you is new benchmark construction, per-fixture power analysis and CI design, contamination methodology beyond the ingest signals, and the quarterly capability report. Chapter 06 details the four handoffs.
- **`ai-risk-engineer` (peer, level 25)** — authors the harm model whose red-team fixtures live in the catalogue the same way per-team fixtures do; the plane runs them and reports verdicts into the safety-plane audit stream (mod-411).
- **`agentic-safety-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — ships red-team findings that often become fixture rows via the trace-to-catalogue promotion path (chapter 04 → chapter 03). Your contract is to accept their findings as fixture rows and to surface per-fixture pass rates they can read as a safety signal.
- **`ai-infra-sre` (peer) and your own future mod-410 work** — consume the gate-decision stream and the nightly leaderboard as operational signals; the mod-410 incident-to-fixture back-feed is the primary way regression fixtures grow over time.
- **`agentic-ai-engineer` (level 30) and `senior-agentic-ai-engineer` (peer, level 40)** — the primary customers. They author the agents the plane evaluates; their contract to you is "I will not bypass the gate"; your contract to them is "the gate will not be noise". The paved-road SDK (mod-412) packages the plane's eval surface for them.
- **`agentic-systems-architect` (peer)** — pairs with you on the eval methodology surface quarterly; the surface moves faster than this module can commit to and the review is a joint responsibility.
